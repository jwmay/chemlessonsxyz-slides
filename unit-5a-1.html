CHEMICAL REACTIONS
What Is a Chemical Reaction?
The process by which the atoms of one or more substances are rearranged to form different substances is called a chemical reaction.
Chemists use equations to represent chemical reactions.
Reactants are the substances you start with. Products are the substances formed.
⚗️
ATOMS REARRANGE
reactants → products
CHEMICAL REACTIONS

Reading a Chemical Equation

An equation is a recipe: ingredients on the left, results on the right.
2H2+O22H2O
Reactants
The ingredients — the substances you start with (left of the arrow).
Yields
The arrow separates the reactants from the products.
Products
The results — the substances formed (right of the arrow).
CONSERVATION OF MASS

The Law of Conservation of Mass

Mass is neither created nor destroyed during a chemical reaction — it is conserved.
IN OTHER WORDS
massreactants = massproducts
⚖️ French scientist Antoine Lavoisier was one of the first to use a balance to monitor the mass of chemical reactions.
He heated mercury(II) oxide and monitored the mass before and after the experiment.

INDICATIONS OF A REACTION
Evidence of a Chemical Reaction
To know for certain a reaction has taken place requires evidence that one or more substances have changed identity.
Observations can be made to provide qualitative indications that a chemical reaction has occurred.
Qualitative = described with your senses (what you see, feel, smell) rather than measured with numbers.
🔍
LOOK FOR CLUES
what did you observe?

WRITING EQUATIONS

Word Equations vs. Skeleton Equations

Word equation 💬
Statements called word equations indicate the reactants and products of chemical reactions.
“Aluminum and bromine react to produce aluminum bromide.”
Helpful — but they lack important information.
Skeleton equation 🦴
Uses chemical formulas and symbols rather than words to identify the reactants and products.
Al + Br2 AlBr3
Formulas show exactly what reacts.

WRITING EQUATIONS

Conservation of Mass in Equations

The law of conservation of mass must be maintained: mass of reactants = mass of products.
The same number of each atom must appear on each side of a correct equation.
To equalize the atoms, coefficients are added when necessary.
2H2O
Coefficient — a small whole number in front of a substance; the number of moles of it.
Subscript — part of the formula; tells how many atoms are in one unit.
BALANCING EQUATIONS

Steps for Balancing Equations: 1–3

1
Write the skeleton equation
Correct formulas. Check for the Big 7 — alone, they MUST have a subscript of 2.
›
2
Count reactant atoms
Count each element in the reactants. A polyatomic ion on both sides counts as a single unit.
›
3
Count product atoms
Count the atoms of each element in the products.
BALANCING EQUATIONS

Steps for Balancing Equations: 4–6

4
Add coefficients
Make the number of atoms of each element equal on both sides.
›
5
Reduce
If EVERY coefficient will reduce, rewrite using the simplest ratio.
›
6
Check your work
Recount all atoms on both sides and make sure they are equal. ✅
🚫 NEVER change a subscript to balance an equation — doing so changes the identity of the substance. (H2O is water; H2O2 is hydrogen peroxide!)

BALANCING EQUATIONS

Balancing: Hydrogen + Oxygen

Tally the atoms (reactants | products), add one coefficient, re-tally. Repeat.
1 H2 + O2 H2O H 2 | 2O 2 | 1 O doesn’t match.
2 H2 + O2 2H2O H 2 | 4O 2 | 2 Need 2 waters — O fixed, now H is off.
3 2H2 + O2 2H2O H 4 | 4O 2 | 2 Need 2 H2 — balanced! ✅
