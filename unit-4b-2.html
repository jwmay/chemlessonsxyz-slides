LEWIS STRUCTURES
Molecular Structures
Lewis structures use symbols and bonds to show the relative position of the atoms.
Dashes represent a pair of bonding electrons.
Dots show valence electrons that aren’t shared — nonbonding electrons (lone pairs).
Cl–Cl
DASH = SHARED PAIR
dots = lone pairs

LEWIS STRUCTURES

Step 1: Sum the Valence Electrons

Sum the valence electrons from all atoms. If the structure is an ion:
Negative charge
+1 e⁻ each
Positive charge
−1 e⁻ each
Example: NH4+ = 5 + 4(1) − 1 = 8 valence e⁻
LEWIS STRUCTURES

Step 2: Choose the Central Atom

Decide which atom is the central atom and connect all other atoms to it with a single bond.
Carbon present?
If carbon is present, it is the central atom.
Hydrogen?
Hydrogen is NEVER the central atom.
One unique atom?
If there is one unique atom, it is usually central.
Ex: ClO3− → Cl
Otherwise…
The atom with the lowest electronegativity is usually central.
Ex: HOBr → O

RESONANCE & EXCEPTIONS

Exceptions to the Octet Rule

Fewer than an octet
BF3
B: 3 e⁻ + 3 F: 21 e⁻ = 24. Boron ends with only 6.
BeH2: Be has only 4.
Odd number of electrons
NO
N: 5 e⁻ + O: 6 e⁻ = 11 — one electron can’t be paired.
More than an octet
SF6
S: 6 e⁻ + 6 F: 42 e⁻ = 48. Sulfur holds 12 in six bonds.
VSEPR & SHAPE
Molecular Shapes: VSEPR
Lewis structures show bonds, but they do not indicate the shapes of molecules.
To predict shape we use the VSEPR model — valence-shell electron-pair repulsion.
Electron pairs around a central atom repel each other, which sets the molecular geometry — the arrangement of atoms in space.
🔺
PAIRS PUSH APART
shape = arrangement in space
VSEPR & SHAPE

POLARITY

Polarity & Molecular Shape

A molecule’s shape determines its polarity.
Symmetrical → nonpolar
The pulls on the electrons cancel out.
Ex: CO2, CH4, BF3
Unsymmetrical → polar
The pulls don’t cancel — one end is more negative.
Ex: H2O, NH3, HCl
