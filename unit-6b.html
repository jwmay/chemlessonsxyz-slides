STOICHIOMETRY
What Is Stoichiometry?
Stoichiometry is the study of quantitative relationships between the amounts of reactants used and amounts of product formed by a chemical reaction.
In other words: the mathematics of chemical equations — how much reacts, and how much is made.
🧮

STOICHIOMETRY
Review: Balanced Equations
Chemical equations indicate the reactants and products involved in a chemical reaction.
The coefficients in a balanced equation indicate the number of particles of each substance taking part in the reaction.
Coefficients can be read as particles — or as moles.
⚖️
BALANCE FIRST
coefficients count particles
STOICHIOMETRY

Two Ways to Read an Equation

N2H4 + 2H2O2 N2 + 4H2O
AS MOLECULES 🔬
1 molecule of hydrazine reacts with 2 molecules of hydrogen peroxide to produce 1 molecule of nitrogen and 4 molecules of water.
AS MOLES ⚖️
1 mole of hydrazine reacts with 2 moles of hydrogen peroxide to produce 1 mole of nitrogen and 4 moles of water.
MOLE RATIOS

A mole ratio is a ratio between the numbers of moles of any two substances in a balanced equation.

LIMITING REACTANT

Limiting Reactant: Cookies 🍪

Chocolate chip cookies
• 1 cup butter
• ½ cup white sugar
• 1 cup packed brown sugar
• 1 tsp vanilla extract
• 2 eggs
• 2½ cups all-purpose flour
• 1 tsp baking soda
• 1 tsp salt
• 2 cups chocolate chips
Makes 3 dozen cookies
What if we only had one egg — could we make 3 dozen cookies?
No — only 1½ dozen. The egg limits us. 🥚
What if we had 6 eggs and twice as much of everything else — could we make 9 dozen cookies?
No — only 6 dozen. Now the eggs are in excess.
LIMITING REACTANT

Limiting & Excess Reactants

Just like cookies: the amount of product formed depends on the amounts of reactants available.
Excess reactant
🥡
Most of the time we have more of one reactant than we need. That reactant is the excess reactant — there is too much.
Some is left over.
Limiting reactant
🛑
The other reactant limits how much product we get. Once it runs out, the reaction stops. This is the limiting reactant.
It decides how much product forms.
LIMITING REACTANT

Finding the Limiting Reactant

1
Balance
Write the balanced equation.
›
2
Pick one product
Choose one product as your unknown — use the same one for each conversion.
›
3
Two mass–mass
Convert EACH reactant’s mass to the mass of your chosen product.
›
4
Smaller wins
The reactant that makes the smaller amount of product is limiting.
The reactant that makes the least amount of product is the limiting reactant. Be sure to pick one product!
LIMITING REACTANT

Example: Find the Limiting Reactant

3.5 g of copper is added to a solution containing 6.0 g of silver nitrate, producing silver and copper(II) nitrate.
Cu + 2AgNO3 Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag
3.5 g Cu1 mol Cu63.5 g Cu2 mol Ag1 mol Cu107.9 g Ag1 mol Ag= 12 g Ag
Cu: excess
6.0 g AgNO31 mol AgNO3169.9 g AgNO32 mol Ag2 mol AgNO3107.9 g Ag1 mol Ag= 3.8 g Ag
AgNO3: limiting
AgNO3 produces the smaller amount of silver, so it is the limiting reactant — only 3.8 g of Ag can form.

PERCENT YIELD
Percent Yield
You have learned how to determine the amount of product that will be produced in a chemical reaction.
In reality, that calculated amount is not always the actual amount obtained from the reaction.
Percent yield compares what you actually got with what you expected.
🎯
EXPECTED vs ACTUAL
how close did you get?
PERCENT YIELD

Calculating Percent Yield

Expected yield 🧮
The amount of a product that should be produced based on calculations.
Actual yield ⚖️
The amount of a product that is actually produced from a chemical reaction.
THE PERCENT OF THE EXPECTED YIELD THAT IS ACTUALLY OBTAINED
Percent yield =actual yieldexpected yield× 100%
PERCENT YIELD

Example: Percent Yield

A piece of copper with a mass of 5.00 g is placed in a solution containing excess AgNO3. The silver metal produced has a mass of 15.2 g. What is the percent yield?

① EXPECTED YIELD
5.00 g Cu1 mol Cu63.5 g Cu2 mol Ag1 mol Cu107.9 g Ag1 mol Ag= 17.0 g Ag

15.2 g17.0 g × 100 = 89.4%
