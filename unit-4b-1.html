Covalent Bonds
The chemical bond that results from sharing electrons is a covalent bond.
A molecule is formed when two or more atoms bond covalently.
Covalent bonds form between nonmetal elements.
🤝

Sharing Electrons: Cl₂

Each Cl has 7 valence electrons. Together they share one pair.

SINGLE, DOUBLE & TRIPLE
Sigma (σ) Bonds
Single bonds form when 1 pair of electrons is shared between 2 atoms.
Single bonds are called sigma (σ) bonds.
The shared pair is centered between the two atoms — along the line connecting the nuclei (the internuclear axis).
σ
ON THE AXIS

SINGLE, DOUBLE & TRIPLE
Multiple Bonds & Pi (π) Bonds
Double bonds: 2 pairs shared  ·  Triple bonds: 3 pairs shared.
Double and triple bonds contain sigma and pi (π) bonds.
A pi bond forms when parallel orbitals overlap; its electron density is above and below the internuclear axis.
π
ABOVE & BELOW

LENGTH & STRENGTH

Bond Strength

The strength of a covalent bond is determined by the energy required to break that bond.
STRENGTH
single < double < triple
⚡The bond-dissociation energy is the energy required to break a bond
↑Greater bond-dissociation energy → stronger bond
+As the number of bonds increases, the bond is stronger and harder to break
LENGTH & STRENGTH

Endothermic vs. Exothermic

Reactions break old bonds (costs energy) and form new bonds (releases energy).
🧊 Endothermic
A greater amount of energy is required to break the bonds in the reactants than is released when new bonds form in the products.
Energy is absorbed
🔥 Exothermic
More energy is released when new bonds form than is required to break the bonds in the reactants.
Energy is released

NAMING

Naming Rules & Exceptions

The rules
Rule 1: Name the first nonmetal with the proper prefix.
Rule 2: Name the second nonmetal with the proper prefix and change the ending to -ide.
The exceptions
The first element never uses the mono- prefix.
If a prefix makes two vowels in a row, one is usually dropped.
CO = carbon monoxide (not monocarbon monooxide)

Names & Formulas of Acids
An acid is a compound that produces H⁺ ions when dissolved in water.
So acid formulas have the general form HX…
…where X is a monatomic or polyatomic ion.
🍋

ACIDS

Naming Acids: 3 Rules

-ide anion
hydro- … -ic

All binary acids are named this way.
