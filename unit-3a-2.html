THE QUANTUM MODEL
The Quantum Model of the Atom
Like the Bohr model, the quantum mechanical model restricts the energy of electrons to certain values.
But it doesn't define a specific path an electron takes around the nucleus.
Instead, it estimates the probability of finding an electron in a certain position, called an orbital.
🎯
PROBABILITY, NOT PATHS
Where is it likely to be?
THE QUANTUM MODEL

Paths vs. Probability

Bohr Model
🪐
Gave the electron specific paths around the nucleus, based on the energy of the electron.
Quantum Model
☁️
We can only guess, within a certain degree of probability, the location of an electron.
Picture a boundary drawn where there's a 90% probability of finding the electron inside the line.
THE QUANTUM MODEL
The Electron Cloud
The probability of finding an electron within a certain volume of space around the nucleus can be shown as a fuzzy cloud called an electron cloud.
The cloud is more dense where the probability of finding the e− is higher.
☁️
DENSER = MORE LIKELY
Fuzzy, not a fixed orbit

ELECTRON CONFIGURATIONS
Electron Configurations
The arrangement of electrons in an atom is called the atom's electron configuration.
Knowing how the electrons are configured helps us predict how atoms interact with other elements.
When atoms come into contact, it's the outer electrons that do the chemistry. 🧪

ELECTRON CONFIGURATIONS

3 Principles for Placing Electrons

Electrons are distributed among levels, sublevels & orbitals in order of lowest energy to highest.
1 · Aufbau
🛋️
Fill the lowest energy orbitals first.
“Lazy Tenant Rule”
2 · Pauli Exclusion
↑↓
Max of 2 electrons per orbital, with opposite spins.
3 · Hund's Rule
🚌
One electron per orbital before pairing.
“Empty Bus Seat Rule”
ELECTRON CONFIGURATIONS · 1

The Aufbau Principle

Electrons fill the lowest energy orbitals first — the “Lazy Tenant Rule.” 🛋️
FILLING ORDER · LOW HIGH ENERGY
1s› 2s› 2p› 3s› 3p› 4s› 3d› 4p› 5s› 4d…
Notice: 4s fills before 3d — the 4s orbital is lower in energy.
ELECTRON CONFIGURATIONS · 2
The Pauli Exclusion Principle
An orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons.
To occupy the same orbital, the electrons must spin in opposite directions.
Depicted with arrows pointed in opposite directions.
↑↓
