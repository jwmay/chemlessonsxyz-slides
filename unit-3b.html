Why Organize the Elements?
By the end of the 1700s, scientists had identified only about 30 elements.
In the 1800s, new lab techniques (like spectroscopy) doubled the number of known elements.
As the list grew, scientists needed better ways to classify the elements.

DEVELOPMENT OF THE TABLE
Newlands' Law of Octaves
In 1865, John Newlands arranged the elements in order of increasing atomic mass.
The properties of the elements repeated every 8 elements.
He called it the law of octaves — after the notes of a musical scale.
🎹
DO RE MI FA SOL LA TI DO
the 8th note repeats the 1st
DEVELOPMENT OF THE TABLE

Mendeleev's Periodic Table

In 1869, Russian chemist Dmitri Mendeleev published the first periodic table.
He arranged the elements by atomic mass, so that elements in the same column have similar properties.
He even predicted the existence of missing elements not yet discovered. 🔮

Periodic Trends
Many properties of the elements change in a predictable way as you move through the periodic table.
These predictable changes are called periodic trends.
Example: the reactivity of alkali metals with water. 🎬

PERIODIC TRENDS
Atomic Radius
Atomic radius: the distance from the center of an atom's nucleus to its outermost electron.
In other words — how big the atom is.
⭕
NUCLEUS OUTER e−
the size of the atom
PERIODIC TRENDS

Atomic Radius: The Trend

Down a group
Size increases
Because of an increase in principal quantum number — a 3s orbital is larger than a 2s orbital.
Across a period
Size decreases
Nuclei gain more protons and pull the electrons in closer to the nucleus.

Ionic Size

Cations (+ ions)
➕
Decrease in size
Electrons are lost, an orbital is vacated, and repulsive forces are reduced.
Anions (− ions)
➖
Increase in size
Electrons are gained, which increases repulsive forces — electrons move farther from the nucleus.
PERIODIC TRENDS
Why Form Ions? The Octet Rule
Atoms want a stable electron configuration.
For all elements (except H and He), this means having 8 valence electrons.
This is called the Octet Rule.

PERIODIC TRENDS
Ionization Energy
Ionization energy is the energy needed to remove an electron.
High ionization energy the atom holds onto its electrons very tightly.
Low ionization energy more likely to lose electrons and gain a positive charge.
🧲
UNITS: kJ/mol
energy to pull off an e−
PERIODIC TRENDS

Ionization Energy: The Trend

Down a group
Decreases
The outer electron is farther from the nucleus, so it is easier to remove.
Across a period
Increases
More protons hold the electrons more tightly.
Notice it's the opposite of the atomic radius trend.
