HISTORY OF ATOMIC THEORY

Two Greeks, Two Ideas

DemocritusGreek philosopher · 460–370 B.C.
⚛️
Proposed that all matter is made of tiny, individual particles he called “atomos.”
His ideas were met with criticism from other philosophers.
AristotleGreek philosopher · 384–322 B.C.
🏛️
Disagreed with Democritus — he did not believe empty space could exist.
His views went unchallenged for 2,000 years, until science developed methods to test them.

Early Ideas about Matter
Aristotle and many other philosophers believed all matter was composed of four elements.
It was also commonly accepted that matter could be endlessly divided into smaller and smaller pieces.

HISTORY OF ATOMIC THEORY

Dalton’s Atomic Theory (1808)

John Dalton — English schoolteacher, 1766–1844 — revived Democritus’ idea with evidence:
1
🔬
Matter is made of extremely small particles called atoms.
2
🧱
Atoms are indivisible and indestructible.
3
👯
Atoms of a given element are identical in size, mass & properties.
4
🆚
Atoms of one element are different from those of another.
5
🧩
Atoms combine in whole-number ratios to form compounds.
In a chemical reaction, atoms are separated, combined, or rearranged — never created or destroyed.

When an electric charge is applied, a ray of radiation travels from the cathode (−) to the anode (+) — called a cathode ray.

HISTORY OF ATOMIC THEORY
Discovery of the Electron
In 1897, using a cathode ray tube, J.J. Thomson detected a negatively charged particle.
He received the Nobel Prize in 1906 for identifying the first subatomic particle — the electron.
English physicist, 1856–1940.

HISTORY OF ATOMIC THEORY

Rutherford’s Gold Foil Experiment

Alpha particles were fired at a thin sheet of gold foil.
Alpha particles are positively charged helium nuclei.
Particle hits on the detecting screen (film) are recorded.
MOST passed straight through
A FEW were deflected
VERY FEW bounced way back
“Like howitzer shells bouncing off of tissue paper!”

HISTORY OF ATOMIC THEORY

Discovery of the Neutron

⚪
In 1932, Rutherford’s coworker James Chadwick showed the nucleus also contained another subatomic particle — the neutron.
🏅
In 1935, Chadwick received the Nobel Prize in Physics for proving the existence of neutrons.
English physicist, 1891–1974.

Does mass number change?
