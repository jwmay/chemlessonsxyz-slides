F Falcon Chemistry
CHEMICAL BONDS
Chemical Bonding
Atoms are generally found in nature combined, held together by chemical bonds.
A chemical bond is a force that holds two atoms together — an electrical attraction between the nuclei and valence electrons of different atoms.
There are two types of chemical bonds: ionic and covalent.
🔗
TWO TYPES OF BONDS
ionic · covalent
CHEMICAL BONDS
Ionic Bonds
Ionic bonds result from the transfer of electrons from one atom to another.
The result is an ionic compound — a compound composed entirely of ions.
Always between a positive ion (cation) and a negative ion (anion).
🔁

Properties of Ionic Compounds

⚖️

Electrolytes

WRITING FORMULAS

Writing Ionic Formulas: 4 Rules

1
Cation first
Always write the cation first, then the anion. It’s better to be positive than negative! 😄
NaCl
2
Neutral overall
Ions combine in the simplest whole-number ratio so the charges add up to zero.
+2 + (−2) = 0
3
Criss-cross
If charges don’t cancel, criss-cross the charges to get the subscripts.
AlCl3
4
Parentheses
Put parentheses around a polyatomic ion when you need more than one of it.
Al(OH)3

WRITING FORMULAS

When Charges Already Cancel

Some ionic compounds’ charges automatically cancel each other out — one of each ion.
K++I−KI
Mg2++O2−MgO
WRITING FORMULAS

Rule 3: The Criss-Cross Method

If charges do not cancel, criss-cross the charge numbers to get the subscripts.
Al3++Cl−AlCl3
Ca2++P3−Ca3P2
Check: (+3) + 3(−1) = 0 and 3(+2) + 2(−3) = 0 ✓  Always use the simplest whole-number ratio.
WRITING FORMULAS

Rule 4: Parentheses

Add parentheses around a polyatomic ion when more than one of it is needed.
Na++SO42−Na2SO4
Al3++OH−Al(OH)3

Naming Ions: Monatomic Cations

Cations formed from metal atoms have the same name as the metal.
Na+=sodium ion
Zn2+=zinc ion
Al3+=aluminum ion
If a metal forms more than one charge, show it with Roman numerals in parentheses.
Fe2+=iron (II)
Fe3+=iron (III)
What would you call Cu+ and Cu2+? 🤔
NAMING
The Older Way: -ous & -ic
An older method still widely used adds the suffix -ous or -ic to the root of the element’s Latin name.
-ous = the lower charge  ·  -ic = the higher charge
Fe2+ = ferrous ion  ·  Fe3+ = ferric ion
🏛️
FERRUM = IRON

Metals With More Than One Charge

Determine the charge on the metal, then show it as a Roman numeral.
FeCl2=iron (II) chloride
FeCl3=iron (III) chloride
PbBr2=lead (II) bromide
Work backwards: each chloride ion is Cl−, so two of them means the iron must be Fe2+ iron (II).
NAMING

Naming With Polyatomic Ions

1) Name the cation (Roman numeral if needed).  2) Name the polyatomic ion — don’t change its suffix.
Fe(OH)2=iron (II) hydroxide
Ca3(PO4)2=calcium phosphate
Na2SO4=sodium sulfate

METALS
Metallic Bonds & Metals
Although metals are not ions, they share some properties with ionic compounds.
The bonding in metals is based on the attraction of particles with unlike charges.
Metals often form lattices in the solid state, similar to ionic crystal lattices.
🔩
METALLIC BONDING
cations + mobile electrons

Properties of Metals

Melting & boiling points
🌡️
Moderately high, with some exceptions (mercury).
Malleable, ductile, durable
🔨
Cations are mobile but strongly attracted to the electrons — not easily removed.
Hardness & strength
💪
Increase as the number of delocalized electrons increases.
Conduct heat & electricity
⚡
The movement of mobile electrons makes metals good conductors.
METALS

Alloys

Alloy: a substance that contains a mixture of elements and has metallic properties. Two types:
Substitutional
🎺
Some host metal atoms are replaced by other metal atoms of similar size.
Ex: Brass (copper + zinc)
Interstitial
🏗️
Small atoms fill some of the holes in the closest-packed metal structure.
Ex: Steel (iron + carbon)
